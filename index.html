Dear reader,

Thanks for opening this. I'm Alina, and I study computer science at Columbia, with minors in financial engineering and math.

Most of my time goes to research. When a language model gets an early step of its reasoning wrong, every step built on it can go wrong too, so I study which steps are worth checking when you can't afford to check all of them. Across about 15,000 claims from 8 models, a step's position in the reasoning graph turned out to be a better guide than the model's own confidence. The paper is in progress, and I presented a poster on it at the Jane Street and Columbia Research Fair this year.

When I'm not doing research, I'm usually building something, often at a hackathon. A couple of those are enclosed below.

I also TA Intro to Python and teach English with the Arctic Reading Circle. On weekends I'm usually walking around Manhattan, trying a new food spot, or losing at tennis and pickleball.

If you'd like to talk, I'd be glad to hear from you.
